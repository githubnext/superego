Superego halo — six variations

Variations on direction C from superego-icon.html: a ring over a dot. Each varies angle, weight, float or halo form, and each splits into two layers (halo and head) for Icon Composer. Ego is the head alone.

Variants 6
A

Classic

Optimizes for balance: the one you picked, with the halo and head given equal weight and a clear gap. · Tradeoff character: it's the neutral baseline, so it has no particular attitude.

Variant A · icon, sizes, Ego mark

What works

Reads instantly at every size, and the head and halo split cleanly into two Icon Composer layers. Proportions are already close to the first icon's visual weight.

What risks

It's the obvious version; anyone else drawing a halo icon lands here too.

Pick this if

You want the reference version to refine in Icon Composer.

B

Tilted

Optimizes for whimsy: the halo is knocked about 14° askew, a conscience worn at a jaunty angle. · Tradeoff calm: the asymmetry makes it a little restless, and next to Ego it reads more cheeky than wise.

Variant B · icon, sizes, Ego mark

What works

One small rotation adds the most personality of any variation. It says "rules, but with a sense of humour", and it survives at 16px.

What risks

Tilts can look like a rendering mistake. It also gets harder to centre optically, because the halo pulls the eye right.

Pick this if

You want the slight note of whimsy front and centre.

C

Hairline

Optimizes for elegance: a thin halo floating high over a smaller head, with lots of air. · Tradeoff small sizes: at 16px the hairline is under a pixel and the halo flickers or vanishes.

Variant C · icon, sizes, Ego mark

What works

It’s the most refined and premium-looking, and closest to a Swiss poster. It looks wonderful in Liquid Glass at large sizes.

What risks

The menu bar and Dock at small sizes lose the halo, leaving just a dot (which is Ego, not Superego).

Pick this if

The big icon matters most and you'll draw a heavier hint for small sizes.

D

Bold & tight

Optimizes for small-size punch: a thick halo sitting close above a bigger head, a compact silhouette. · Tradeoff lightness: it's heavier and less floaty, and the halo starts to look like a hat brim or lid.

Variant D · icon, sizes, Ego mark

What works

It’s the strongest at 16 and 32px and the most legible in the Dock. The silhouette is distinctive even when blurred.

What risks

Less heavenly: the gap is what makes a halo float, and this one barely has one.

Pick this if

Recognition at a glance matters more than delicacy.

E

Nimbus

Optimizes for distinctiveness: a flat disc halo behind the head, as in medieval icons, instead of a ring seen from the side. · Tradeoff immediacy: fewer people read a ring behind a head as a halo; some see a keyhole, a person icon or a pawn.

Variant E · icon, sizes, Ego mark

What works

Less expected than the ellipse, and the round forms echo the squircle. The knockout gap gives it a crafted, printed feel.

What risks

It drifts towards a generic "user" avatar, and it's the closest thing to a profile picture placeholder.

Pick this if

You want a halo nobody else has, and like the art-history wink.

F

Glowing

Optimizes for atmosphere: a soft light around the halo, made for Icon Composer's glass, specular and blur. · Tradeoff flatness: it depends on effects, so the tinted and clear modes and the tiny sizes all behave differently.

Variant F · icon, sizes, Ego mark

What works

It’s the most alive, and the tinted version glows. Icon Composer does this well natively (blur and translucency on the halo layer only).

What risks

Glows age quickly and can look like a 2010 skeuomorphic icon. At 16px it just looks blurry.

Pick this if

You plan to lean on Liquid Glass effects rather than pure shape.

How to pick a direction

Check the 16px ladder first, then the hero icon. Whichever wins goes to Icon Composer as two layers.
