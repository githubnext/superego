Superego app icon — five directions

The app is Superego, and its own agent is Ego. Each direction keeps one idiom (a monochrome glyph on a white glass squircle) so the pick drops straight into resources/superego.icon, and each comes with a matching Ego mark.

Variants 5
A

Ψ, the psyche letter

Optimizes for continuity: same serif, the same single-letter idea as the first icon, and Ψ is the shorthand for psychology itself. · Tradeoff specificity: Ψ says "psychology" in general, not Superego in particular, and it's a popular mark (Psi, PSI, countless clinics).

Variant A · icon, sizes, Ego mark

What works

Swapping one Greek glyph for another keeps everything you already have: the Icon Composer file, the serif and the black-on-white glass. Lowercase ψ for Ego is a neat parent/child pair.

What risks

It's the least surprising option and leans on the alphabet rather than on the name. At 16px Ψ reads as a trident or fork.

Pick this if

You want the rename to feel like the same app with a new name tag.

B

Superscript e

Optimizes for wit: the name as typography. An ego that sits above its own baseline is literally super-ego. · Tradeoff small sizes: the ghost and the baseline turn to mush at 16px, leaving a lone "e".

Variant B · icon, sizes, Ego mark

What works

It’s the most like the first icon in spirit: one serif glyph doing clever work. Ego is the same e sitting on the line, so the pair explains itself when shown together.

What risks

A big serif "e" invites confusion with Edge, Evernote, Emacs and e-anything. It has two elements where the first icon has one, so it's busier.

Pick this if

You like the joke and are happy for the mark to be quiet rather than iconic.

C

Halo

Optimizes for whimsy and recognisability: a conscience floating over a head, readable at every size. · Tradeoff gravitas: it tips towards saint, angel or "good boy", which is lighter in tone than the name.

Variant C · icon, sizes, Ego mark

What works

It holds up best at 16px, and Ego as the bare dot is the clearest pairing of the five: the agent is the dot, the app is the dot with something watching over it.

What risks

The halo can read as "innocent" or "religious" more than "rules and standards". The ellipse gets fiddly in Liquid Glass layers.

Pick this if

You want the friendliest mark, with a wink of humour.

D

Iceberg

Optimizes for meaning: Freud's own diagram, with a visible tip and the mass of the mind under the waterline. · Tradeoff freshness: icebergs are a slide-deck cliché, and they also mean "hidden danger".

Variant D · icon, sizes, Ego mark

What works

It tells the most story: an agent shows a little and does a lot below the surface. The tinted variant looks good with the translucent lower mass.

What risks

The model actually puts the ego at the tip, so a strict reader sees the wrong part named. The thin waterline drops out below 32px.

Pick this if

You want the icon to explain the name to someone who half-remembers Psych 101.

E

Container

Optimizes for the product's structure: the app is the frame and the agent is the dot inside it. Superego holds Ego. · Tradeoff personality: a rounded square inside a rounded square reads as generic "app", "stop" or "record".

Variant E · icon, sizes, Ego mark

What works

It's the most literal fit for your naming (app = Superego, meta-agent = Ego), and Ego as the dot is clean. It’s very crisp at small sizes.

What risks

There’s no psychology and no wit; it could be any utility. A square-in-a-squircle is a common mark.

Pick this if

You want the system diagram, not the pun.

How to pick a direction

The glyph matters most at 32px and below, so compare the ladders before the hero icons.
