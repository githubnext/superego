Showing focus without the square border

The work area gets room around it, and the thread and each pane become rounded sheets like the side panel. Five ways to say which one has the keyboard.

Shared by all five. The row of columns gets 8px of room on every side and between columns: the same 8px the side panel is inset by. Each column is a sheet with an 18px radius (the 26px window less its 8px inset), so the columns, the panel and the window are concentric. Columns sit on a new --ground, a step below the panel's grey (#e9e9ed / #151517), so the panel and the sheets both read in light and dark.

This is how the columns looked at first: f77ad2cc “Panes of their own widths, as cards with room around and between” drew them as 10px cards with 8px around and between on the sidebar's grey. d022c33e later put them edge to edge on one sheet, and 3eece21a drew focus as the 2px square border.

Also shared. The title bar's tab for the focused column is raised, as on the branch today. With the list focused, its selected row turns accent-tinted (it's grey otherwise), as a macOS sidebar does.

The columns' row clips what overflows it, so a ring or shadow must fit inside the 8px of room. Each preview respects that.

A

Ring

Optimizes for the clearest signal, closest to today and to the macOS focus ring · Tradeoff an accent outline is always on screen, even while you only type

Variant A · a soft accent ring on the rounded sheet

What works

Reads at a glance at any column count and in both modes: a 1px accent line with a 4px halo, following the corners. It's the macOS focus ring at region scale, and the list gets the same ring around the panel.

What risks

It's still a border, only rounder and softer. Colour on the frame competes with the accent in the content (send button, links, the diff). The halo needs the full 8px of room.

Pick this if

Focus has to be unmistakable (keyboard-heavy use, many panes) and you're happy to keep an outline, just a kinder one.

B

Recede

Optimizes for the strongest read with no accent at all · Tradeoff what you're reading beside your work is half as legible

Variant B · the unfocused columns fade back

What works

The clearest at a glance with four columns: one sheet is lit, the rest sit back. No colour, no line, nothing new on screen; calm once you're typing.

What risks

Panes exist to be read beside the thread: a diff or a page at half strength while you write about it is a real cost. With the list focused, every column fades. A web page or terminal in a pane must be dimmed too, which a native web view may not allow.

Pick this if

Panes are mostly glanced at, and you want focus to read like a spotlight rather than a mark.

C

Lift

Optimizes for the most native, quietest signal · Tradeoff the weakest at a glance, in light mode above all

Variant C · the focused sheet raised

What works

Feels like macOS: the key thing sits a little higher, everything else stays as it is and fully legible. In dark it lifts the way macOS does, a step lighter, not by shadow alone.

What risks

In light, a shadow that fits in 8px of room is subtle: with three white sheets you may have to look for it. The list has no lift of its own (it's already raised), so only the row's tint says it has focus.

Pick this if

You mostly know where you are (you clicked there) and want the window as calm as possible.

D

Lit head

Optimizes for leaving every sheet's body untouched · Tradeoff the thread has no head of its own, so it says it differently

Variant D · the accent in the pane's tab and the title bar's

What works

The accent lives where you name things: the pane's tab tints and its name turns accent, and the title bar's tab matches, so the two agree. Bodies, edges and shadows never change.

What risks

The thread has no tab, so its composer wears the accent edge instead: two signals for one idea. A thin strip at the top of a tall column is easy to miss with your eyes on the text.

Pick this if

You want a quiet signal that is unmistakable once you look for it, and you'd consider giving the thread a head like the panes'.

E

Lift and step back

Optimizes for a clear read that stays calm, all text at full strength · Tradeoff three small signals to tune together

Variant E · C's lift, the other sheets a step toward the ground, the title bar's tab lit

What works

The focused sheet is the only white (or lightest) one and the only one with a shadow; the others go a step toward the ground, text untouched. The accented tab in the title bar names it. Strong at four columns, no colour on any frame.

What risks

Unfocused sheets are no longer the sheet colour, so a pane's own background (a web page, an image) shows a seam. Three moving parts, though each is a token: ground, lift, tab.

Pick this if

You want B's glanceability without dimming what you read, and C's calm without having to look for it. My pick.

How to pick

I recommend E: it reads at a glance with four columns in both modes, adds no colour to any frame, and leaves every column fully legible. A is the fallback if a step of grey turns out too quiet on some displays.

Unmistakable →A. Ring: today's signal, rounded and softened.
No accent, strongest →B. Recede, if dimmed panes are acceptable.
Calmest →C. Lift, if a subtle shadow is enough.
Bodies untouched →D. Lit head, ideally with a head for the thread too.
Clear and calm (recommended) →E. Lift and step back.
