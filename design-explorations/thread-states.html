Today

Every place that shows a thread uses StatusGlyph, a 7px dot, except the menu bar, the CLI, the board and the transcript, which each have their own marks.

  • Colour alone separates the states that want you. Finished, needs you and failed are the same filled dot in blue, orange and red. In ⌘U's highlighted row all three turn into the same white dot.
  • Working looks different in each place: a hollow ring that pulses in the sidebar, a filled dot with a halo in the transcript, ● in the menu bar and CLI. Idle is a filled grey dot in the app and ○ in the CLI.
  • Working and finished share the accent. The only difference is a 1.5px ring versus a fill, and the ring fades in and out.
  • One shape, two meanings: the dashed ring means waiting for sign-in in the sidebar and queued on the board. A hollow ring means working, MCP off and planned. Orange means needs you, and on the board it also means stalled.
  • Quiet children lie about being unread. When one finishes it gets the blue “finished” dot, but the bell, the Dock and ⌘U don't count it. The row says unread and the count says it isn't.
  • Some states have no mark at all: quiet (a child reporting to its parent), from an outside agent, stalled (only the board knows), and a folded parent whose children are busy. An offline machine just dims to 40%. In Settings, “offline” is a red failed dot.
  • The words drift: Finished / done / landed; Needs you / Waiting / Waiting for sign-in; * for finished in the menu bar; the row shows a time for finished and working threads alike.
A

Shaped dots

Optimizes for the smallest change that fixes everything: still one small dot per thread, but each state has its own shape. · Tradeoff at 7px the shapes are subtle; the dot stays a code you learn, not something you read.

Variant A · sidebar, rail, title bar, ⌘U · light and dark

What works

Filled means “look at me”, and only that. A spinner means work, and every spinner turns in step, so ten of them move like one and don't flicker. A spinner that stops (orange) is stalled. Every shape also works in white on ⌘U's blue row.

What risks

The target and the no-entry dot need a second look at rail size. Spinners are the most motion of the four directions, even in step.

Pick this if

You like today's quiet dots and want them honest: shape first, one meaning per mark, the same everywhere.

B

Small symbols

Optimizes for reading a state at a glance: a 14px circle with a ✓, ?, ! or lock in it, the way Linear and GitHub do it. · Tradeoff louder and busier: every row gets an icon, and the sidebar starts to look like an issue tracker.

Variant B · sidebar, rail, title bar, ⌘U · light and dark

What works

Nothing to learn: ? asks, ! failed, ✓ finished, a lock wants permission. It works in grayscale, in white, and in the menu bar's text (✓ ? !), so the CLI and the app finally match.

What risks

Idle rows get a ring too, so every row has a mark and the list gets heavier. Filled symbols in colour pull hard, maybe too hard for a sidebar you look at all day.

Pick this if

You'd rather be told than have to remember, and you run many threads where telling question, permission and failure apart at a glance matters.

C

Words first

Optimizes for calm and plain language, like a mail app: unread titles are semibold with a dot, working is three dots breathing where the time goes, and what a thread waits on is said in a word. · Tradeoff it needs room for words, so the rail and small places fall back on a second set of glyphs.

Variant C · sidebar, rail, title bar, ⌘U · light and dark

What works

The quietest list of the four: idle and working rows have no glyph at all, and unread is as plain as in Mail. “Question”, “Permission” and “Sign in” say what's wanted, with nothing to decode.

What risks

Two vocabularies, words on rows and dots in the rail, so it's less “one language”. The wave sits at the far right, where your eye looks last.

Pick this if

The sidebar should feel like a calm inbox, and you read rows more than you scan the rail.

D

Heartbeat

Optimizes for motion that means something: a working thread is a grey ring that flashes blue each time it actually does something. A stuck one goes still, then turns into a flat orange ring. · Tradeoff needs a beat per activity (there's activeAt to go on), and still rings look alike at a glance.

Variant D · sidebar, rail, title bar, ⌘U · light and dark

What works

Ten working threads twinkle now and then instead of spinning together. You can feel which one is busy and which has gone quiet, and “stalled” becomes a state every list shows, not only the board. The static shapes are A's.

What risks

Between beats, working is a grey ring, close to idle at a glance. Beats need a feed of activity in every window, and a long model call with no tool calls looks idle.

Pick this if

Watching many agents is the job, and spotting the stuck one matters more than knowing they're all busy.

How to pick a direction

The shapes, the motion and the words can be mixed: D's motion works on A's shapes, and C's semibold unread title works with any of them.

Read it, don't learn it →B, small symbols

Recommendation

A's shapes, with C's semibold title for unread. It's the smallest change from today and fixes every inconsistency on the list. Shape carries the meaning in grayscale and in ⌘U's white row, and it works out to the menu bar and CLI as text: · ◐ ● ◉ ⊖ ◌. Keep D's “stopped spinner = stalled” as a later step, once the window knows when activity last happened.

Decisions for you

  1. The shapes: shaped dots (A), or symbols in circles (B)? Everything else follows from this.
  2. How working moves: spinners turning in step (A/B), a breathing three-dot wave (C), or a flash per real activity (D)? Under reduced motion, all of them stand still.
  3. Quiet children: a smaller grey spinner while they work, and no “finished” dot when they finish, since their parent hears and the count already agrees? A quiet child that needs you still shows the target.
  4. The words: keep Finished · Needs you · Failed · Waiting for sign-in (A, D), or name what's asked on the row: Question · Permission · Sign in (C)? Either way, the menu bar, CLI and notifications use the same words as the app.

Not in the data yet: a mark for a thread started by an outside agent (the title bar chip and row mark here are a proposal), stalled outside the board (D), and the folded parent's roll-up, which comes with “Threads list: collapse projects, sections and sub-threads”. A thread is in one state at a time, so “working and unread” only happens on a folded parent whose child finished: the roll-up shows it.
